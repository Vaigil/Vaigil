Add or change access

Pick somebody this store already knows, or name a new person. Typing a name that is nearly right is how a grant ends up applying to nobody and looking as though it applies to somebody.

A role is a rung: a viewer reads the queue, an analyst triages and hunts, a responder runs cases and approves somebody else’s acts, an admin manages people, connectors and settings. A job is a different kind of work: an engineer tunes detections and automations, a vuln-manager decides vulnerabilities, an auditor reads the queue and the log for a set time. A permission is one of those decisions, granted alone.

Two different questions

Grants decide what somebody is allowed to do. Credentials decide what they can do right now. Removing a grant does not invalidate a token already in somebody’s hand — the token carries its own role, which is what makes a narrow token narrow. Removing somebody means doing both, and revoking a credential also revokes every session minted from it.

There is no session table here to inspect: signing in produces a bearer token and the token is the session. “Active now” means used within the last {{.ActiveWithin}} — the closest honest answer to who is logged in.
